§5.2 — Statistical heterogeneity alone gives limited optimiser separation
                                                                  TARGET 900 w
================================================================================

CLAIM
  Statistical heterogeneity alone produces small, fragile, and runtime-sensitive
  FedAvg/FedProx separation that does not, on its own, justify a default
  recommendation.

REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
  Table 5.2   Six rows: IID, Engineered (pure-PyTorch), Engineered (Flower),
              Dirichlet α=0.1, Specialist 1-of-7, Node-pinned L4.
  Figure 5.2  Forest plot of Δ(FP−FA) across the six settings — MUST GENERATE
              at thesis_ready/figures/F_statistical_heterogeneity_forest.pdf.

EVIDENCE (5)
  • IID negative control gives Δ = −0.0068 over n=10 [v4]; the two methods are
    functionally indistinguishable and this is not a meaningful FedProx penalty.
  • Engineered partition brackets at 0.01–0.03 across code paths: pure-PyTorch
    Δ = +0.0267 (9/10 paired wins) [v1], Flower paired Δ = +0.0069 [v2], Flower
    μ-peak Δ = +0.0131 [v3]; the Flower paired number is the central headline
    because it carries git_commit and update-norm logs [v71], the pure-PyTorch
    headline does not [v70].
  • Partition robustness is uniformly small: Dirichlet α=0.1 Δ = +0.0079 [v45],
    specialist 1-of-7 Δ = +0.0081 [v46], node-pinned L4 multi-seed Δ = +0.005
    (▲ NOT −0.006) [v47]; the ladder L4 single-seed Δ = −0.030 is driven almost
    entirely by one vascular F1 drop of −0.220 [v49] while all other classes
    stay within |Δ| ≈ 0.03 [v50].
  • Variance and convergence on the engineered partition: FedProx test SD drops
    from 0.025 to 0.014 (45% lower) [v51–v53], reaches val macro-F1 = 0.40 in
    9.8 vs 12.7 rounds [v54–v56], and shows a mid-training peak gap of +0.094
    at round 50 [v57] — but these trajectory characteristics are tested only on
    the engineered partition.
  • Dirichlet provenance: legacy analysis CSVs were stale relative to the raw
    JSONs; the thesis uses the regenerated aggregates and does not cite the
    seed-8675309 catastrophic-failure claim from earlier drafts.

§5.3 — μ sensitivity: the proximal term helps only in a narrow range
                                                                  TARGET 500 w
================================================================================

CLAIM
  Small μ can modestly help and large μ harms performance — there is no
  universal "more heterogeneity → larger μ" rule on this benchmark.

REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
  Table 5.3   Five rows (μ ∈ {0, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0}) with macro-F1 ± SD,
              Δ vs FedAvg, paired wins out of 10, and a one-word interpretation.
  Table 5.3b  (optional in-main, recommended) Per-class μ sensitivity at L4 —
              7 classes × 5 μ values; bridges to §5.6 if included.
  Figure 5.3  μ inverted-U curve; existing
              thesis_ready/figures/F_mu_sensitivity_outcome_and_convergence.pdf
              should be checked for clean inverted-U presentation before reuse.

EVIDENCE (4)
  • 10-seed μ-sweep at the engineered partition gives FA baseline 0.493 → μ=0.001
    0.504 → μ=0.01 0.506 (peak) → μ=0.1 0.479 → μ=1.0 0.419 [v3]; the curve is
    a clear inverted-U with the peak at μ=0.01.
  • Δ vs FedAvg at the catastrophic end (μ=1.0) is −0.074, dominated by rare-
    class deficits whose per-class breakdown belongs in optional Table 5.3b;
    above μ=0.1 the proximal term already pushes performance below FedAvg.
  • The μ-peak Δ of +0.013 lies inside the same small-effect band as the Flower
    paired Δ of +0.007 from §5.2, so the two are reconciled as one modest,
    code-path-sensitive effect rather than two distinct phenomena.
  • The "stronger heterogeneity → larger μ" practical expectation from Li et
    al. is not supported on this benchmark; this is a benchmark observation,
    not a refutation of the underlying theoretical analysis.

TRANSITION
  "Because the proximal term itself is sensitive and sometimes harmful, the
  larger FedProx gains under straggler protocols require a separate explanation."

§5.4 — Under Li-style straggler asymmetry, FedProx's advantage is mediated
       mainly by γ-inexact partial-update handling      ★ CENTREPIECE  1,100 w
================================================================================

CLAIM
  Under Li-style straggler asymmetry, the large FedProx advantage is mediated
  mainly by γ-inexact partial-update handling; the proximal term in isolation
  contributes ≈ +0.004 macro-F1 once drop-handling is matched.

REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
  Table 5.4   Four conditions at L4: FA-no-strag, FA+drop, FP+drop, FP+γ-inex;
              columns include macro-F1, Δ vs FA+drop, and which mechanism is
              isolated; arithmetic decomposition (+0.004 prox-only, +0.111
              γ-inex) printed under the table.
  Table 5.4b  Same schema at L1 (NEW — cross-partition control); decomposition
              line under the table: prox-only −0.006, γ-inex +0.012, drop-cost
              −0.001.
  Figure 5.4  thesis_ready/figures/F_val_curves_extreme_gaps.pdf, Panel A only
              (FA+drop and FP+drop validation curves overlap through 150 rounds).
              Panel B (perfect-storm) moves to appendix C.

EVIDENCE (5)
  • Removing statistical heterogeneity from the straggler protocol REVERSES the
    advantage rather than merely shrinking it: system_het_iid_random Δ = −0.013
    [v34] vs system_het_random Δ = +0.017 [v35] — the FedProx straggler-regime
    advantage requires both statistical and system heterogeneity simultaneously.
  • Four-condition decomposition at L4 isolates the mechanisms cleanly: FA+drop
    0.364 [v6], FP+drop 0.368 [v7], FP+γ-inex 0.479 [v8] — total gap +0.115
    [v11] decomposes as proximal-only +0.004 [v9] plus γ-inexact +0.111 [v10],
    i.e. ~96% of the gap is the γ-inexact mechanism, not the proximal term.
  • Perfect-storm magnification preserves the same ordering at much larger
    magnitude but is NOT a clean causal decomposition: FA+drop val macro-F1 is
    flat at 0.087 ± 0.049 for all 150 rounds (model selected at round 1 — the
    untrained init beat every trained checkpoint) [v12], FP μ=1.0 0.365 [v14],
    FP μ=0.01 0.491 [v13]; several settings change at once across these cells.
  • L1 cross-partition control isolates the necessary condition: under quantity-
    only heterogeneity the decomposition flattens — FA-no-strag 0.601 [v38],
    FA+drop 0.599 [v39], FP+drop 0.593 [v40], FP+γ-inex 0.605 [v41]; γ-inexact
    contributes only +0.012 [v43] vs +0.111 at L4, drop-cost is −0.001 [v44]
    vs −0.120 at L4, and proximal-only is −0.006 [v42]. The γ-inexact mechanism
    operates only when the dropped client carries informationally unique class
    signal — quantity-only skew gives it nothing to rescue. (This contrast
    between Table 5.4 and Table 5.4b is the centrepiece's strongest single
    piece of evidence for the mechanism interpretation.)
  • Visual confirmation: F_val_curves_extreme_gaps Panel A shows FA+drop and
    FP+drop trajectories overlap throughout 150 rounds — the proximal term
    alone does not change the learning curve under matched drop handling.

TRANSITION
  "The straggler experiments show one form of system asymmetry. The next
  section asks whether a different asymmetry type changes the optimiser
  ranking."

§5.5 — Learning-rate asymmetry reveals FedNova regime-dependence
                                                                  TARGET 850 w
================================================================================

CLAIM
  FedNova is robust under tested learning-rate asymmetry but fails under random-τ
  straggler heterogeneity; the optimiser ranking depends on which axis of system
  heterogeneity is exercised.

REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
  Sub-display  6-row LR envelope table (ratios × {FA, FP, FN, ΔFN−FA}), in-text,
               not numbered.
  Sub-display  FedNova-regime contrast (LR-asymmetry → robust, random-τ →
               collapse), in-text.
  Figure 5.5   thesis_ready/figures/F_asymmetric_lr_L4.pdf — MUST REGENERATE
               to include the new 10:1, 20:1, 50:1 ratios; two panels (macro-F1
               and rare-class F1 vs LR ratio, log-spaced x-axis 1:1 to 50:1).
  Figure 5.6   thesis_ready/figures/F_heterogeneity_escalation.pdf — placed at
               the END of this section, synthesising §5.2, §5.4, §5.5.

EVIDENCE (5)
  • Six-ratio macro envelope (n=3 per cell): at 1:1 FA/FP/FN = 0.457/0.491/0.509
    [v15], at 50:1 = 0.367/0.367/0.498 [v20]; FedAvg and FedProx plateau at the
    rare-class floor by 5:1 and cannot degrade further, while FedNova stays in
    0.49–0.53 across the whole envelope and |Δ vs its own 1:1 baseline| never
    exceeds 0.024.
  • At 50:1 the FedNova–FedAvg gap is +0.131 macro-F1 [v21], the largest single-
    axis system-asymmetry gap measured in the thesis.
  • Rare-class collapse is severe and asymmetric across optimisers: FA/FP rare-F1
    drops to 0.016/0.010 by 10:1 [v22] and to ≈0.003 by 50:1; FedNova holds
    rare-F1 at 0.33–0.41 throughout [v23] — the τ-normalisation mechanically
    preserves the smaller client's effective contribution.
  • FedNova random-τ collapse (its OWN paragraph): mean macro-F1 = 0.237 across
    10 seeds [v32], with 3 seeds (s31337, s271828, s161803) collapsing to
    majority-class prediction at macro_f1 = 0.114 [v33] — this is a regime
    outside Wang 2020's stable-τ assumption, where τ-normalisation amplifies
    stragglers' noisy partial updates instead of correcting for them. FedNova's
    robustness is therefore regime-specific in BOTH directions.
  • Heterogeneity-escalation synthesis (closes the section): the sequence IID
    → L4 symmetric → Li 2020 §5.2 → perfect-storm μ=1.0 → perfect-storm μ=0.01
    yields within-pair Δs of −0.006 → −0.006 → +0.115 → +0.278 → +0.404 — a
    monotone rise that summarises how regime severity, not optimiser identity,
    drives the optimiser gap.

OVERCLAIMING GUARD
  Do not write "FedNova is best." Write "FedNova is more robust under the
  tested LR asymmetry, but not under all forms of system heterogeneity."

TRANSITION
  "These regime-dependent optimiser differences matter because the weak
  protocols fail in a clinically specific way: they lose rare-class sensitivity."

§5.6 — Rare-class collapse into mel-nevi, and why FedProx is not simply an
       imbalance corrector                                        TARGET 750 w
================================================================================

CLAIM
  Across failing protocols, rare and minority classes collapse systematically
  into mel-nevi; loss-side rebalancing (weighted CE) competes with or exceeds
  FedProx on its own headline metric.

REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
  Figure 5.7   thesis_ready/figures/F_cross_experiment_per_class.pdf — per-class
               diagonal heatmap as the headline figure.
  Sub-display  3-column in-text table at §5.6.3: rows FedAvg/FedProx, cols
               CE / Weighted CE / Focal; each cell macro-F1 ± SD (rare F1).
  Optional appendix figures: D1 5:1 confusion matrices, collapse trajectory.

EVIDENCE (5)
  • Per-class collapse spans three orders of magnitude across optimisers at the
    same LR asymmetry: at D1 5:1 the melanoma F1 is FedAvg 1.20% [v58], FedProx
    6.13% [v59], FedNova 46.34% [v60] — clinically the same disease, three
    different detection regimes.
  • Misrouting to mel-nevi runs from approximately 24% (best protocol) to
    approximately 67% (perfect-storm FA+drop); the exact figures require per-
    protocol confusion-matrix aggregation and should be re-derived before
    citing (NOT verified to the guide's two-decimal precision in this pass).
  • Two trajectories of the same failure mode: instant collapse when the rare-
    class client is dropped from aggregation (perfect-storm FA+drop flat at
    0.087); gradual collapse when rare-client updates are accepted but
    progressively overwhelmed (D1 5:1).
  • Loss-side comparators at L4 (n=3): FA+CE 0.477 (rare 0.285) [v24], FA+wCE
    0.505 (rare 0.367) [v25], FA+focal 0.467 (rare 0.317) [v26]; FP+CE 0.478
    (rare 0.291) [v27], FP+wCE 0.503 (rare 0.346) [v28], FP+focal 0.476 (rare
    0.304) [v29] — across all six (algorithm × loss) cells, FedProx adds
    essentially nothing on top of the loss-side intervention.
  • FedAvg-only decomposition: plain CE → wCE = +0.029 macro-F1 [v30]; plain
    CE → focal = −0.010 macro-F1 but +0.032 rare-F1 [v31] — wCE and focal
    trade off macro against rare differently, and FedProx improves neither
    materially in this L4 symmetric setting; FedProx should be understood as
    a drift/protocol intervention, not a direct class-imbalance correction.

OVERCLAIMING GUARD
  Do not write "Weighted CE makes FedProx useless." Write "In this L4 setting,
  FedProx adds little on top of weighted CE."

TRANSITION
  "These class-level failures concentrate in the optimiser and protocol cells
  where update activity at the rare-class client is suppressed; the next
  section examines that mechanism directly."

§5.7 — Mechanism and robustness checks                            TARGET 500 w

CLAIM

  protocol-flip rates show that large regime-driven effects are reliable while
  small symmetric-protocol effects are not.

REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
  Figure 5.8   thesis_ready/figures/F_update_norms.pdf (engineered trajectories)
               with optional cross-protocol scatter panel.

EVIDENCE (5)
  • Engineered update norms: FedAvg mean ‖Δw‖ = 1.523 vs FedProx 1.260 [v61–v62]
    — a reduction of approximately 17%; ▲ the "1.03 / 33% reduction" figure in
    earlier drafts uses a different aggregation rule that does not reproduce
    from the raw client_update_norms_*.csv files and must be re-derived before
    citing.
  • Cross-protocol pooled analysis (78 L4 runs across 8 algorithm × protocol
    cells): Spearman r = +0.634 between dominant-client update-norm magnitude
    and rare-F1 [v64]; the relative-influence ratio of the rare-class-holding
    client correlates near zero across protocols (r = −0.045) [v65].
  • Within asymmetric_lr alone, relative-influence DOES track rare-F1
    (r = +0.80 FedAvg, +0.87 FedProx) [v66–v67] — within-protocol ordering
    does not extrapolate across protocols; this disanalogy must be stated
    explicitly and the cross-protocol claim labelled exploratory, not causal.
  • Protocol-flip audit: 0/9 large-effect seed comparisons flip (perfect-storm,
    Li 2020 asymmetric, asymmetric μ — all 0/3) [v68]; 3/9 small-effect
    comparisons flip (node_pinned_L4 s42, extended_rounds_L3 s42,
    two_client_90_10 s456) [v69] — conclusions must be weighted by effect
    magnitude, and sub-1pp differences in symmetric settings are reporting-
    protocol-fragile.
  • Moved to appendix E: B-dissimilarity proxy, training instability metric,
    asymmetric μ Yao test, early-warning detector, small-hospital case study —
    each kept clearly labelled as exploratory.

§5.9 — Limitations                                                TARGET 200 w
================================================================================

CLAIM
  Findings are bounded by dataset choice, federation size split, optimiser
  comparator space, metric set, and seed budget — not by analysis effort.

REQUIRED ARTEFACTS
  None (prose-only).

EVIDENCE (5)
  • Single dataset at low resolution (DermaMNIST 28×28) and mixed federation
    sizes (7-client for headline / statistical-heterogeneity; 2-client for
    mechanism probes) — the thesis makes no cross-scale generalisation claim
    between the two federation sizes.
  • Fixed local-epoch budget E=20 and simulated straggler protocols (epoch
    counts, not wall-clock deadlines); n=3 for several strong asymmetry claims
    (Li 2020, perfect-storm, asymmetric LR) and n=1 for the heterogeneity-
    ladder pilot.
  • Runtime divergence between pure-PyTorch and Flower on the engineered
    partition (≈ +0.027 vs +0.007) is itself a limitation on magnitude claims.
  • No SCAFFOLD / FedAdam / FedYogi comparator; no AUC / calibration metrics
    (argmax predictions only).
  • Per-client validation distributions were not logged centrally, so q-FFL-style
    fairness CDFs and Sheller-style cross-client generalisation grids cannot
    be produced from the existing artefacts.

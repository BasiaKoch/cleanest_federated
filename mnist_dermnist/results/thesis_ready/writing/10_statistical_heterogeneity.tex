\section{Statistical Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:results-statistical-het}

Statistical heterogeneity refers to differences in the data distributions
that the participating clients hold locally. In the medical-imaging
context this is the dominant source of non-i.i.d.\ behaviour: each
institution sees a different case mix, and the resulting per-client
class priors $p_i(y)$ diverge from the global prior $p(y)$. The proximal
term in FedProx is theoretically motivated precisely by this regime
(Li et al.\ 2020): when the local objectives $F_i$ disagree at the
broadcast iterate $w^t$, the proximal anchor
$(\mu/2)\lVert w - w^t \rVert^2$ damps client drift and the global
update remains closer to the consensus that minimises the federated
objective $F$.

This section tests the FedProx-vs-FedAvg comparison under four
distinct partition designs of DermaMNIST, each chosen to expose a
different facet of label heterogeneity. We motivate each partition,
visualise its class distribution, and report the paired-seed
inferential and descriptive results separately. The headline
\texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients} partition is the pre-registered
primary analysis, run on the pure-PyTorch reference loop. The three
other partitions (IID, Dirichlet $\alpha = 0.1$, specialist) are
evaluated on the Flower simulation runtime as descriptive robustness
analyses; the headline partition is also replicated on Flower for the
cross-runtime sensitivity check (Section~\ref{sec:cross-runtime}).

\subsection{Experimental design and partition motivation}
\label{sec:partition-designs}

Each of the four partition designs corresponds to a specific question
about the FedProx mechanism. The class-distribution visualisations are
shown in Figure~\ref{fig:partition-distributions}.

\paragraph{IID partition (Figure~\ref{fig:partition-distributions}a) ---
falsification check.}
Each of the seven clients receives a uniform random sample of $\sim
1001$ training images drawn i.i.d.\ from the global DermaMNIST pool.
The per-client class distribution mirrors the global distribution
exactly in expectation, with the majority class (melanocytic nevi,
67\%) appearing in every client at proportional volume. Under this
partition the local objectives $F_i$ are i.i.d.\ samples of the same
underlying distribution; their gradients at any $w^t$ are unbiased
estimators of $\nabla F(w^t)$ and the bounded-dissimilarity assumption
of Li et al.~(2020) is satisfied in the limit. The proximal anchor
therefore has no client drift to constrain, and FedProx is predicted to
provide no advantage over FedAvg ($\Delta \approx 0$). A statistically
significant positive $\Delta$ under IID would \emph{falsify} the
drift-control mechanism interpretation; the IID arm thus functions as
a null-condition probe of the broader thesis claim.

\paragraph{Dirichlet $\alpha = 0.1$ partition
(Figure~\ref{fig:partition-distributions}b) --- literature-standard
non-i.i.d.}
The Dirichlet-partitioning protocol of Hsu et al.~(2019) is the
de-facto standard for synthetic non-i.i.d.\ benchmarking in federated
learning. For each class $c$ a vector
$\mathbf{q}_c \sim \mathrm{Dir}_7(\alpha \mathbf{1})$ is drawn and the
class-$c$ samples are allocated to the seven clients in proportions
$q_{c,1}, \dots, q_{c,7}$. The concentration parameter $\alpha = 0.1$
produces highly skewed per-client priors: in the realisation shown,
client~6 receives 3{,}721 samples (mostly nevi, $n = 3{,}538$) while
client~4 receives only 91 (chiefly basal-cell carcinoma, $n = 78$).
This partition combines label skew with severe quantity skew --- the
two axes of statistical heterogeneity introduced in
Kairouz et al.~(2021) --- and is the regime in which most of the
non-i.i.d.\ FL benchmarks report FedAvg degradation. Including it in
the experimental matrix demonstrates that the headline result is not
specific to our engineered partition.

\paragraph{Specialist partition (Figure~\ref{fig:partition-distributions}c)
--- singleton ownership, pairing-lever check.}
Every minority class is assigned to exactly one client; the majority
class (melanocytic nevi) is split across all seven clients with
unequal shares chosen so that the per-client sample counts $n_i$ match
the engineered balanced-paired partition exactly
($n_i \in \{673, 963, 964, 1094, 1095, 1108, 1110\}$). The specialist
design isolates the \emph{paired-co-training} feature from a generic
label-skew effect: under the engineered partition every minority
class is held by two clients (which must drift apart during local SGD
and be reconciled at aggregation), whereas under the specialist
partition each minority class is held by exactly one client (no
inter-client drift exists for the proximal anchor to reconcile on that
class). The contrast between the two partitions therefore isolates
the structural mechanism that the proximal anchor is hypothesised to
exploit. This pre-registered counterfactual is the strongest defence
against the engineered-partition critique discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:headline-limitations}.

\paragraph{Balanced paired partition (Figure~\ref{fig:partition-distributions}d)
--- engineered mechanism experiment (headline).}
The headline partition is engineered to satisfy three desiderata
simultaneously: (i) every minority class is held by exactly two
clients, creating co-trained drifting pairs that the proximal anchor
must reconcile; (ii) the majority class melanocytic nevi is held by
all seven clients (it provides a within-experiment per-class control
where the proximal anchor cannot help, against which the
minority-class effects can be compared); and (iii) per-client sample
counts $n_i$ are within a $1.65 \times$ range
($\{673, 963, 964, 1094, 1095, 1108, 1110\}$) so that quantity skew is
mild and the label-skew axis is isolated. Three of seven clients
co-train on \emph{actinic keratoses + basal cell carcinoma}, two on
\emph{benign keratosis + dermatofibroma}, two on \emph{melanoma +
vascular lesions}; one client holds only nevi. This composition is
the unique partition (up to permutation of client identifiers) that
satisfies all three desiderata and supports a clean per-class
mechanism prediction.

\subsection{Primary analysis: headline paired comparison}
\label{sec:headline-result}

The headline experiment runs the FedProx-vs-FedAvg paired comparison
on the engineered \texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients} partition
through the pure-PyTorch sequential reference loop. The protocol uses
ten paired seeds $\{42, 123, 456, 789, 999, 2024, 31337, 161803,
271828, 8675309\}$, with identical partition, identical initial
weights, identical per-(round, client) dataloader RNG, and identical
optimiser state at $\mu = 0$ across the two algorithms. Within-pair
differences therefore reflect only the algorithmic difference (the
gated proximal term), not random-state divergence. Hyperparameters are
fixed at $E = 20$ local epochs, $R = 150$ communication rounds,
learning rate $0.01$, momentum $0.9$, $\mu = 0.01$, and full
participation $C = 1.0$.

\paragraph{Proximal coefficient $\mu$.}
The proximal coefficient $\mu = 0.01$ was chosen a~priori on the
basis of Li et al.~(2020), where this value is reported as a robust
default across their experimental matrix. A sparse exploratory check
on a subset of seeds at $\mu \in \{0.001, 0.1\}$ (pure-PyTorch,
engineered partition; $n = 1$ at $\mu = 0.001$, $n = 2$ at
$\mu = 0.1$) is consistent with $\mu = 0.01$ being near the local
optimum: $\mu = 0.1$ underperformed $\mu = 0.01$ on both seeds
tested (mean decrement $-0.030$ macro-F1), while $\mu = 0.001$ was
within $0.012$ macro-F1 of $\mu = 0.01$ on the single seed where
both were evaluated. The sample sizes are too small for formal
inference, so the $\mu = 0.01$ choice is reported as pre-registered
rather than empirically optimised, but the available exploratory
data do not contradict the literature default.

Table~\ref{tab:headline-summary} reports the paired-seed inferential
suite. The mean within-pair difference is
$\Delta = +0.0267 \pm 0.0349$ (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank
two-sided $p = 0.020$; exact Walsh-average 95\% CI
$[+0.002, +0.053]$; Hodges--Lehmann estimator $+0.022$; FedProx wins
9 of 10 paired seeds). The result is robust to leave-one-out
re-sampling: all 10 LOSO subsamples remain significant at
$\alpha = 0.05$. The Walsh-average lower confidence bound
($+0.002$) approaches the pre-registered smallest effect of interest
$\mathrm{SES} = 0.01$ but excludes zero. We therefore report the
headline result as a \emph{small but statistically supported}
FedProx advantage; the effect is not negligible relative to the SES
but neither is it large in absolute terms compared to the centralised
upper bound's distance from federation.

\subsubsection{Per-class breakdown}
\label{sec:per-class}

The per-class decomposition (Table~\ref{tab:per-class-holm}, paired
Wilcoxon with Holm--Bonferroni correction over the family of seven
classes) localises where the FedProx advantage arises. Only the
melanoma class survives multiplicity correction at family-wise
$\alpha = 0.05$ ($\Delta = +0.114$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.041$);
actinic keratoses is the next closest ($\Delta = +0.067$,
$p_{\mathrm{raw}} = 0.020$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.117$) but loses
significance after correction. The clinically critical melanoma class
is therefore the single per-class anchor of the headline result. The
majority class melanocytic nevi shows essentially no effect
($\Delta = +0.002$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 1.000$) --- the partition
holds nevi in every client, so no inter-client drift exists on this
class for the proximal anchor to reconcile. The per-class
trajectories (Figure~\ref{fig:per-class-convergence}) confirm the same
pattern visually: the largest mid-training gaps occur on melanoma
(panel E) where FedAvg fails to learn the class beyond
$\mathrm{F1} \approx 0.06$ by round 50 while FedProx has reached
$\mathrm{F1} \approx 0.30$, whereas the nevi trajectories are
essentially superimposed.

\subsection{Robustness across partition designs}
\label{sec:partition-robustness}

The three additional partitions (IID, Dirichlet $\alpha = 0.1$,
specialist) are run on the Flower simulation runtime and reported
descriptively in Table~\ref{tab:partition-dose-response}. None of the
four Flower-runtime per-partition contrasts reaches $\alpha = 0.05$
in our sample. The IID partition has the smallest (and slightly
negative) mean $\Delta = -0.007$, consistent with the theoretical
prediction that the proximal anchor has nothing to constrain when
local objectives are i.i.d.\ samples of the same distribution.
Dirichlet $\alpha = 0.1$ ($+0.008$), specialist ($+0.008$), and the
engineered partition ($+0.007$) cluster within a $\pm 0.005$ band of
each other --- under the Flower runtime, varying partition heterogeneity
intensity does not systematically increase the FedProx advantage at
$n = 10$ paired seeds.

The pre-registered specialist counterfactual (cf.\
Section~\ref{sec:partition-designs}) finds that the specialist
partition's $\Delta$ ($+0.008$) is descriptively smaller than the
engineered partition's ($+0.027$ pure-PyTorch, $+0.007$ Flower).
Direct seed-paired comparison across the two partitions is not
applicable because the partitions differ in client--class assignment
(although the per-client $n_i$ are matched), but the descriptive
ordering is consistent with the pre-registered prediction: pairing
amplifies the FedProx mechanism beyond what generic single-class
specialisation produces.

\subsection{Cross-runtime sensitivity}
\label{sec:cross-runtime}

The headline experiment is replicated on the Flower simulation runtime
on the same engineered partition with the same ten paired seeds. The
two runtimes implement bit-equivalent local objectives at $\mu = 0$
(verified by the cross-runtime equivalence sweep in
Section~\ref{sec:equivalence-check}) and use identical hyperparameters.
Under the pure-PyTorch runtime the within-pair difference is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{PT}} = +0.027$ (paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.020$); under
the Flower runtime it is $\Delta_{\mathrm{Flower}} = +0.007$
(paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.432$); the across-seed mean difference is
therefore $\sim 0.020$ smaller under Flower.

However, the per-seed gap is \emph{not} consistent across the ten
seeds: five seeds show $\Delta_{\mathrm{PT}} > \Delta_{\mathrm{Flower}}$
and five show $\Delta_{\mathrm{Flower}} \ge \Delta_{\mathrm{PT}}$,
and the paired Wilcoxon test on the gap itself yields $p = 0.32$.
Table~\ref{tab:cross-runtime} reports the two single-runtime tests
side by side together with the per-seed gap inference. The
cross-runtime attenuation is therefore a mean-level descriptive
observation, not a per-seed-consistent systematic effect; we describe
it as a cautionary reproducibility observation rather than as a
formal methodological finding. Section~\ref{sec:discussion-runtime}
discusses the plausible sources of seed-level variance (Ray-actor
isolation, floating-point non-associativity in aggregation, parameter
serialisation round-trips) without claiming any one mechanism is
demonstrated.

\subsection{Federation tax}
\label{sec:federation-tax}

To anchor the absolute magnitudes, we additionally train a centralised
non-federated reference on the pooled 7{,}007-sample training set
under the same architecture, optimiser, and evaluation protocol.
Table~\ref{tab:federation-tax} reports test macro-F1 for the
centralised reference and for the four FL configurations at the
matched ten paired seeds. The centralised mean
$0.560 \pm 0.024$ is the upper bound. Pure-PyTorch FedAvg recovers
$86.0\%$ of centralised performance and pure-PyTorch FedProx
$90.7\%$; Flower FedAvg recovers $88.6\%$ and Flower FedProx $89.9\%$.
The within-runtime gap between FedAvg and FedProx ($\sim 4.7$ percentage
points pure-PyTorch, $\sim 1.3$ percentage points Flower) is small
relative to the federation tax itself ($\sim 9$--$14$ percentage
points). The headline FedProx advantage is therefore best understood
not as ``federated training matches centralised'' but as ``federated
FedProx closes a modest portion of the federation gap that federated
FedAvg leaves open.''

\subsection{Summary and limitations of the statistical-heterogeneity arm}
\label{sec:headline-limitations}

\paragraph{Summary.}
The pre-registered primary analysis on the engineered
\texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients} partition under pure-PyTorch
yields a statistically supported FedProx advantage
($\Delta = +0.027$, paired Wilcoxon $p = 0.020$, Walsh 95\% CI
$[+0.002, +0.053]$, LOSO 10/10) that is, at the per-class level,
attributable to a Holm-corrected significant improvement on the
melanoma class ($\Delta = +0.114$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.041$).
Three robustness analyses on alternative partition designs (IID,
Dirichlet $\alpha = 0.1$, specialist) and one cross-runtime
replication (Flower simulation on the engineered partition) are
descriptively consistent with the headline direction --- all five
Flower-runtime mean $\Delta$ values are non-negative, and the IID
partition's slightly negative descriptive $\Delta$ matches the
theoretical null prediction --- but none of the four Flower-runtime
per-condition tests reach $\alpha = 0.05$ at $n = 10$ paired seeds.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The headline partition is engineered to maximise the proximal-anchor
mechanism; its external validity to real multi-institutional cohorts
is unestablished and explicit. The cross-runtime gap, while large in
the across-seed mean, is not consistent at the per-seed level and is
therefore reported as a cautionary observation rather than a
systematic methodological effect. The Flower-runtime descriptive
$\Delta$ values cluster near the pre-registered smallest effect of
interest of $0.01$ macro-F1 and would require a larger paired-seed
sample (estimated $n \gtrsim 30$ on the basis of the observed within-
seed standard deviation) to reach formal significance at the matched
effect size. Per-class tests beyond melanoma are exploratory; the
melanoma result alone is the Holm-corrected positive cell in the
multiplicity-controlled family.

\subsection*{Scenario 1 — Pre-registered outcome:
$\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} > 0$ but smaller than $\Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}$}
% USE WHEN: HL(Δ_specialist) ∈ (0, HL(Δ_paired)) AND Wilcoxon p < 0.05.
% ============================================================================
The specialist sweep returns a positive within-pair difference,
$\mathrm{HL}(\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}}) = {DELTA_SP_HL}$ with exact
Walsh-average-inversion 95\% CI $[{DELTA_SP_CI_LO}, {DELTA_SP_CI_HI}]$
($n=10$, two-sided Wilcoxon $p = {WILCOXON_P_SP}$), confirming that
FedProx's advantage transfers off the paired structure of
\texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients}. The effect size is smaller than
on the engineered partition ($\mathrm{HL}(\Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}) =
{DELTA_PAIRED_HL}$), consistent with the pre-registered mechanism
prediction at \S\ref{sec:hyp-specialist}: the proximal anchor still
damps individual-client drift under one-client-per-class specialisation,
but the absence of co-trained clients holding the same class removes
the largest source of disagreement at aggregation time and therefore
removes the largest part of the proximal term's anchoring work. Placed
in the four-point dose-response (IID $\approx 0$ $\to$ specialist
$\to$ Dirichlet $\to$ paired), the specialist condition fits between
IID and paired in both direction and magnitude, completing the monotone
trend predicted by the drift-control account.

\subsection*{Scenario 2 — Mechanism story is wrong:
$\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} \approx \Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}$}
% USE WHEN: HL(Δ_specialist) ≈ HL(Δ_paired) AND CIs overlap.
% ============================================================================
The specialist sweep returns $\mathrm{HL}(\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}}) =
{DELTA_SP_HL}$ with 95\% CI $[{DELTA_SP_CI_LO}, {DELTA_SP_CI_HI}]$,
indistinguishable in magnitude from $\mathrm{HL}(\Delta_{\mathrm{paired}})
= {DELTA_PAIRED_HL}$. The pre-registered paired-co-training mechanism
account at \S\ref{sec:hyp-specialist} therefore fails its falsification
check: if the proximal anchor needed paired drifting clients to operate,
the specialist condition should have produced a substantially smaller
$\Delta$, and it did not. The honest reframing is that FedProx's
advantage on \texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients} is not a
paired-co-training phenomenon at all — it is a more general
regularisation effect on noisy non-IID updates, plausibly implicit
learning-rate shrinkage via the quadratic anchor. We weaken the
headline claim accordingly: FedProx improves macro-F1 on DermaMNIST
under label skew, but the mechanism is more aligned with the
$\gamma$-inexact-update bound of \citet{li2020fedprox} than with the
paired-drift narrative that motivated the partition design. The four-
point dose-response (IID $\approx 0$ $\to$ specialist $\approx$ paired
$\to$ Dirichlet) collapses into a two-level pattern: any non-IID label
skew produces a positive $\Delta$, regardless of structural specifics.

\subsection*{Scenario 3 — FedProx advantage is paired-specific:
$\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} \approx 0$}
% USE WHEN: HL(Δ_specialist) ≈ 0 AND Wilcoxon p > 0.05.
% ============================================================================
The specialist sweep returns $\mathrm{HL}(\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}}) =
{DELTA_SP_HL}$ with 95\% CI $[{DELTA_SP_CI_LO}, {DELTA_SP_CI_HI}]$ and
$p = {WILCOXON_P_SP}$ (two-sided paired Wilcoxon, $n=10$). The CI
includes zero and the test does not reject the null. We accept that
under one-client-per-minority-class specialisation, FedProx and FedAvg
are statistically indistinguishable on this dataset --- a finding that
narrows the headline claim substantially. The proximal anchor's
empirical benefit is specific to the regime where two or more clients
hold the same minority class and drift apart during local SGD; under
specialisation, every client's local objective lies far enough from
the global consensus that the anchor either has nothing to reconcile
(no paired drifting partner) or, if anything, slows learning by
constraining what would otherwise be the dominant useful update
direction. The thesis claim is reframed: FedProx helps when clients
holding the same minority class drift apart, not whenever label skew
is present. The four-point dose-response (IID $\approx$ specialist
$\approx 0$ $\to$ Dirichlet $\to$ paired) reveals that the relevant
axis is structural co-training overlap rather than label-skew intensity
\emph{per se}.

% ============================================================================
\subsection*{Scenario 4 — Unexpected reversal:
$\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} < 0$}
% USE WHEN: HL(Δ_specialist) < 0 AND/OR upper CI bound < 0.
% ============================================================================
The specialist sweep returns $\mathrm{HL}(\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}}) =
{DELTA_SP_HL}$ (95\% CI $[{DELTA_SP_CI_LO}, {DELTA_SP_CI_HI}]$, paired
Wilcoxon $p = {WILCOXON_P_SP}$, sign test $p = {SIGN_P_SP}$) — a
negative within-pair difference, meaning FedProx \emph{underperforms}
FedAvg under one-client-per-minority-class specialisation. This was not
predicted at \S\ref{sec:hyp-specialist}; we report it honestly rather
than dismissing it as noise. A plausible mechanism is that when each
minority class is owned by a single client, that client's local
objective is highly specialised and the proximal anchor $(\mu/2)
\lVert w - w^t\rVert_2^2$ over-constrains its update toward the global
parameters --- which encode mostly nevi --- and thereby slows the
specialised client's progress on its own minority class. The paired
condition has a natural escape from this trap: the proximal anchor of
the two clients holding the same class pulls each toward a consensus
that still includes the minority class, not toward a nevi-dominated
global. The reframing for the thesis is more drastic than the other
three scenarios: the proximal anchor is not uniformly beneficial under
label skew; it has a regime of helpful effect (paired co-training) and
a regime of harmful effect (single-owner specialisation), and the
direction depends on the partition's structural geometry rather than
on the amount of skew. The four-point dose-response (IID $\approx 0$
$\to$ specialist $<0$ $\to$ Dirichlet $> 0$ $\to$ paired $> 0$) is
non-monotone in the FedProx advantage, and the thesis discussion
section must accommodate this.

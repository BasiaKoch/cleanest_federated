\section{Statistical Heterogeneity Experiments}\label{sec:stat-het}

This section describes the four-partition experimental matrix used to
probe how the FedProx-over-FedAvg advantage depends on the structure of
inter-client data heterogeneity. Each partition tests a different facet
of the same question: \emph{when does the proximal anchor's
$(\mu/2)\lVert w - w^t\rVert_2^2$ regularisation produce a measurable
improvement over plain size-weighted averaging, and why?}

\subsection{Definition: what we mean by statistical heterogeneity}
\label{sec:stat-het-def}

Federated optimisation theory \citep{li2020fedprox,mcmahan2017fedavg}
distinguishes two heterogeneity axes that violate the IID assumption
underlying centralised SGD's convergence guarantees: \emph{statistical
heterogeneity} (clients hold different data distributions) and
\emph{system heterogeneity} (clients perform different amounts of local
work). This section concerns statistical heterogeneity only;
system-heterogeneity experiments are reported in \S\ref{sec:sys-het}.

\citet{kairouz2021advances} decompose statistical heterogeneity into
four sub-axes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Label skew}: clients have different class priors
        $p_i(y)$. The dominant axis in cross-silo medical FL, where
        each site sees a different patient-population case mix.
  \item \textbf{Feature skew}: the conditional input distribution
        $p_i(x \mid y)$ differs across clients (e.g.\ scanner-specific
        acquisition protocols producing systematically different
        intensity histograms for the same underlying tissue).
  \item \textbf{Quantity skew}: clients have different $n_i$.
  \item \textbf{Concept drift}: the label function $p(y \mid x)$
        itself differs across sites or over time.
\end{itemize}

The DermaMNIST corpus \citep{yang2023medmnistv2} is a single-source
dataset; feature skew and concept drift are absent by construction.
This thesis therefore isolates \textbf{label skew} (with controlled
quantity skew), making any observed FedProx advantage attributable to
the drift-control mechanism rather than confounded with feature-shift
remedies such as FedBN.

\subsection{The four-partition experimental matrix}
\label{sec:stat-het-matrix}

To characterise the structure-dependence of the FedProx mechanism we
evaluate FedAvg and FedProx on four partitions of the same 7\,007-sample
DermaMNIST training set, holding everything else constant: same 10
paired seeds $\{42, 123, 456, 789, 999, 2024, 31337, 8675309, 161803, 271828\}$,
same federation ($K=7$ clients, full participation $C=1.0$, $R=150$
communication rounds, $E=20$ local epochs), same model
(\texttt{DermMNISTCNN} with GroupNorm normalisation, 423\,175 parameters),
same optimiser (SGD with $\eta = 0.01$, momentum $0.9$, no weight decay),
same loss (cross-entropy), same $\mu = 0.01$ for FedProx, and same
test-at-best-validation evaluation protocol.

The four partitions, ordered by increasing alignment with the FedProx
proximal-anchor mechanism, are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{IID} (\S\ref{sec:stat-het-iid}) — null control:
        every client receives a uniform random sample from the global
        distribution.
  \item \textbf{Specialist} (\S\ref{sec:stat-het-specialist}) —
        each minority class held by \emph{exactly one} client.
  \item \textbf{Dirichlet $\alpha = 0.1$}
        (\S\ref{sec:stat-het-dirichlet}) — each class allocated to
        clients in proportions drawn from a Dirichlet prior; the
        field-standard synthetic non-IID benchmark.
  \item \textbf{Balanced paired} (\S\ref{sec:stat-het-paired}) —
        each minority class held by \emph{exactly two} clients;
        the engineered headline partition.
\end{enumerate}

This ordering is not merely categorical: \S\ref{sec:stat-het-doseresp}
explains how the four partitions form a measured dose-response over
the structural-heterogeneity axis the proximal anchor is sensitive to.

\subsection{Partition 1: \texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients}
            (engineered headline)}
\label{sec:stat-het-paired}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The headline partition is engineered to maximise the expected
FedProx advantage. The mechanism it tests is \emph{paired
co-training drift}: when two clients hold samples of the same
minority class but optimise their local objectives separately, they
drift apart in parameter space during local SGD; the proximal anchor
$(\mu/2)\lVert w - w^t\rVert_2^2$ penalises that drift and pulls
their updates back toward a shared consensus at aggregation. Without
such co-trained pairs, the proximal term has no consensus to find.

\paragraph{Specification.}
Each minority class (\emph{actinic\_keratoses}, \emph{basal\_cell\_carcinoma},
\emph{benign\_keratosis\_like\_lesions}, \emph{dermatofibroma},
\emph{melanoma}, \emph{vascular\_lesions}) is owned by exactly two
clients. The majority class (\emph{melanocytic\_nevi}) is held by
all seven clients to prevent single-class collapse during local
training. Per-client sample counts:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
\toprule
Client & Specialty pair & Background & $n_i$ \\
\midrule
C0 & actinic + basal      &  670 nevi & 964 \\
C1 & actinic + basal      &  670 nevi & 963 \\
C2 & benign + dermato     &  670 nevi & 1\,095 \\
C3 & benign + dermato     &  670 nevi & 1\,094 \\
C4 & melanoma + vascular  &  670 nevi & 1\,110 \\
C5 & melanoma + vascular  &  670 nevi & 1\,108 \\
C6 & --- (generalist)     &  673 nevi & 673 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The exact per-class counts are listed in
\texttt{data/partition.py} as \texttt{BALANCED\_PAIRED\_7\_CLIENTS\_SPEC};
the partitioner verifies on every load that the spec sums match the
actual class totals.

\paragraph{Pre-registered hypothesis.}
\textbf{H1} (\S\ref{sec:hypotheses}): on \texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients}
at $E=20$, $R=150$, $\mu=0.01$, FedProx improves test macro-F1 over
FedAvg in a paired-seed comparison, tested via two-sided paired
Wilcoxon signed-rank at $\alpha = 0.05$, $n = 10$ seeds.

\paragraph{Anticipated objection.}
A reviewer can argue this partition is engineered to favour FedProx
and that the headline result is an artefact of the pairing structure.
The specialist partition (\S\ref{sec:stat-het-specialist}) is the
empirical counter-argument.

\subsection{Partition 2: \texttt{iid\_7\_clients} (falsification control)}
\label{sec:stat-het-iid}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The drift-control mechanism predicts that absent client drift, the
proximal anchor has nothing to anchor against. IID partitioning is
the cleanest empirical realisation of "no drift": every client
receives a random uniform shard of the global data, so per-client
empirical risks $F_i$ converge to the global $F$ in expectation, and
local SGD steps are unbiased estimators of the global gradient.

If FedProx significantly outperforms FedAvg under IID, the
mechanism story is wrong: the observed headline advantage would
have to be re-attributed to a more generic regularisation effect
(such as implicit learning-rate shrinkage from the quadratic
anchor), and the paired-drift mechanistic claim would be falsified.

\paragraph{Specification.}
\texttt{iid\_7\_clients(labels, seed)} in \texttt{data/partition.py}
performs uniform random sharding:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
perm = rng.permutation(len(labels))
clients = [list(c) for c in np.array_split(perm, 7)]
\end{lstlisting}

Each client receives $\sim$1\,001 samples; per-client class priors
match the global prior in expectation.

\paragraph{Pre-registered prediction.}
$|\Delta_{\mathrm{IID}}| \leq 0.01$ macro-F1 (the smallest effect of
interest, $\mathrm{SES}$), and the Wilcoxon two-sided $p > 0.05$.
The exact-Walsh-average confidence interval for the
Hodges--Lehmann estimate of $\Delta_{\mathrm{IID}}$ should be
contained in $[-0.01, +0.01]$.

\paragraph{Statistical framing.}
Failure to reject the null on IID is the success case here. We
adopt an equivalence-test framing rather than the usual
"non-significant $\Rightarrow \Delta \approx 0$" shorthand: a
$p > 0.05$ does \emph{not} prove $\Delta = 0$, only that the data
do not warrant rejecting the null. The pre-registered effect-of-
interest threshold of $\pm 0.01$ macro-F1 is what would constitute
genuine evidence of equivalence with the no-effect prediction.

\subsection{Partition 3: \texttt{dirichlet\_alpha01\_7\_clients}
            (external validity)}
\label{sec:stat-het-dirichlet}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The \texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients} partition is bespoke
to this thesis. To establish that any observed FedProx advantage
transfers off the engineered partition, we evaluate the same
algorithms on the \emph{field-standard} synthetic non-IID benchmark:
Dirichlet partitioning at $\alpha = 0.1$ \citep{hsu2019dirichlet}.
This is the partitioning protocol adopted by NIID-Bench
\citep{li2022niidbench}, FedBN \citep{li2021fedbn},
SCAFFOLD \citep{karimireddy2020scaffold}, and the FedAdam family
\citep{reddi2021adaptive}, among others. It is not engineered to
favour any particular algorithm.

\paragraph{Specification.}
For each class $c \in \{1, \dots, C\}$, sample a 7-dimensional
proportion vector $\bm{q}_c \sim \mathrm{Dir}_7(\alpha\bm{1})$ with
$\alpha = 0.1$, then allocate class-$c$ samples to the 7 clients
in proportions $q_{c,1}, \dots, q_{c,7}$. The concentration
parameter $\alpha$ controls heterogeneity: small $\alpha$ (e.g.\
$0.1$) yields highly skewed partitions in which each client holds
only a few classes; large $\alpha$ approximates the IID limit.
A re-draw safeguard (maximum 50 attempts) ensures no client is
empty. The partition is seed-deterministic: same seed $\to$
identical Dirichlet draw $\to$ identical client assignment.

\paragraph{Caveats of Dirichlet partitioning.}
Two well-documented limitations are worth flagging
\citep{hsu2019dirichlet,li2022niidbench}: (i) per-class sampling can
produce empty-client classes that need to be re-rolled; (ii) the
resulting per-client quantities $n_i$ are themselves random,
coupling label skew with quantity skew unless explicit
re-balancing is applied. The thesis reports the random per-seed
$n_i$ rather than fixing them, in keeping with the field standard.

\paragraph{Pre-registered prediction.}
$\Delta_{\mathrm{Dir}} > 0$ (the FedProx advantage transfers) but
$|\Delta_{\mathrm{Dir}}| < |\Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}|$ (the engineered
partition still produces the largest effect, because random
Dirichlet allocation does not maximise the paired-co-training
mechanism in the way \texttt{balanced\_paired} does).

\subsection{Partition 4: \texttt{specialist\_7\_clients}
            (engineered-partition defence)}
\label{sec:stat-het-specialist}

\paragraph{Motivation: defusing the engineered-partition objection.}
The headline partition pairs every minority class across two clients.
A reviewer can argue the +$0.027$ macro-F1 advantage observed on
\texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients} is an artefact of this pairing
structure rather than evidence of a general FedProx benefit under
label skew. We pre-register a sister partition that flips the
pairing lever while holding every other confound constant.

\paragraph{Specification.}
Each minority class is owned by \emph{exactly one} client; nevi
is distributed across six specialty clients in unequal shares
chosen so that the per-client $n_i$ matches
\texttt{balanced\_paired} \emph{exactly}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\toprule
Client & Specialty (single class) & Nevi share & $n_i$ \\
\midrule
C0 & actinic       (228) & 736  & 964   \\
C1 & basal         (359) & 604  & 963   \\
C2 & benign\_kerat (769) & 326  & 1\,095 \\
C3 & dermato       (80)  & 1014 & 1\,094 \\
C4 & melanoma      (779) & 331  & 1\,110 \\
C5 & vascular      (99)  & 1009 & 1\,108 \\
C6 & --- (generalist)    & 673  & 673   \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The per-client totals 964/963/1095/1094/1110/1108/673 are
\emph{identical} to those of \texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients}.
This is by design: quantity skew is held constant; only the
structural lever (paired vs.\ specialist) differs. A side-by-side
$\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}}$ vs.\ $\Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}$
contrast therefore isolates the pairing structure from any
quantity-imbalance confound. The same 10 paired seeds drive both
partitions; the federation, model, optimiser, and Flower runtime
are unchanged.

\paragraph{Pre-registered hypothesis
        (timestamp 2026-05-21, SHA \texttt{9f2bb94}).}
\textbf{Direction}: $\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} > 0$.
\textbf{Magnitude}: $|\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}}| < |\Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}|$.
The proximal anchor still damps individual-client drift relative to
the global model under one-client-per-class specialisation, but the
absence of paired co-trained clients removes the largest source of
disagreement at aggregation time.

\paragraph{Four pre-registered outcomes and their interpretations.}
The thesis commits in advance (\texttt{specialist\_partition\_scenarios.tex}, SHA
\texttt{89673f4}) to four mutually-exclusive outcome scenarios and to
the discussion paragraph each implies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} > 0$ but smaller in magnitude
        than $\Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}$ — the pre-registered case;
        mechanism story intact.
  \item $\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} \approx \Delta_{\mathrm{paired}}$ —
        mechanism story falsified; thesis claim must broaden to
        generic-regularisation framing.
  \item $\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} \approx 0$ — FedProx benefit
        is specifically a paired-co-training phenomenon; thesis
        claim narrows to that condition.
  \item $\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}} < 0$ — unexpected reversal;
        discuss whether single-owner specialisation creates a regime
        where the anchor harms learning.
\end{itemize}
The pre-written discussion paragraph for each outcome is in
\texttt{writing/specialist\_partition\_scenarios.tex}; the operator
selects the matching paragraph based on the observed
$\Delta_{\mathrm{specialist}}$ rather than retro-fitting prose to the
result.

\subsection{Predicted behaviour of FedAvg and FedProx across the four
            partitions}
\label{sec:stat-het-behaviour}

\paragraph{FedAvg under each partition.}
FedAvg \citep{mcmahan2017fedavg} minimises the local objective
$F_i(w) = (1/n_i) \sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}_i} \ell(f_w(x), y)$
without any constraint on how far the local iterate may drift from
the broadcast point $w^t$.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{IID}: each $F_i \to F$ in expectation; local SGD
        steps are unbiased estimators of the global gradient; FedAvg
        recovers centralised SGD's convergence rate up to a slowdown
        constant in $E$ and $C$. We expect strong, stable performance.
  \item \textbf{specialist}: each client sees a single minority class
        plus background nevi; $F_i$ deviates strongly from $F$ at
        every $w^t$; local SGD steps are biased toward $F_i$. The
        size-weighted mean of biased updates is not generally a
        descent step for $F$; convergence slows.
  \item \textbf{Dirichlet} $\alpha=0.1$: similar to specialist but
        with random allocation; some seeds produce very severe label
        skew, others milder; per-seed variance in performance is
        higher.
  \item \textbf{balanced\_paired}: similar to specialist in
        per-client class concentration, but with paired co-training:
        two clients hold the same minority class and drift apart
        independently during local SGD. The size-weighted mean
        produces a partial reconciliation, but at each individual
        client the global iterate $w^{t+1}$ may be far from the
        within-pair consensus for that class.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{FedProx under each partition.}
FedProx \citep{li2020fedprox} adds the proximal term
$(\mu/2)\lVert w - w^t \rVert_2^2$ to the local objective with
strength $\mu \geq 0$. The proximal gradient is exactly $\mu(w - w^t)$
(verified by unit test
\texttt{test\_fedprox\_proximal\_term.py::test\_proximal\_gradient\_equals\_mu\_times\_diff}),
penalising drift from the broadcast point.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{IID}: $F_i \approx F$ at every $w^t$, so optimal
        $w \approx w^t$ already; the proximal term is approximately
        zero; FedProx degenerates to FedAvg in this regime.
        We expect $\Delta \approx 0$.
  \item \textbf{specialist}: local SGD pulls $w$ toward $\arg\min F_i$,
        which is highly class-specific; the proximal anchor at $w^t$
        partially undoes this pull. The effect is to dampen individual-
        client drift magnitude but without a paired co-trained partner
        for the anchor to reconcile against. Expected effect: small,
        plausibly within noise.
  \item \textbf{Dirichlet} $\alpha=0.1$: variable; depends on whether
        the random per-seed allocation happens to produce paired-like
        structure or specialist-like structure. Predicted effect is
        positive but smaller in magnitude than balanced\_paired.
  \item \textbf{balanced\_paired}: maximum FedProx leverage. Two
        clients holding the same minority class would each drift
        toward their local $\arg\min F_i$; the proximal anchor pulls
        each toward $w^t$, which is approximately the previous round's
        consensus. The size-weighted mean of two anchor-constrained
        updates finds a better within-pair consensus than the
        unconstrained FedAvg mean. Largest predicted $\Delta$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The four-point dose-response across structural
            heterogeneity}
\label{sec:stat-het-doseresp}

The four partitions form an empirically-derivable dose-response curve.
Rather than describing the partitions categorically ("IID is less
non-IID than balanced\_paired"), we report the
\emph{measured} per-partition heterogeneity score: the mean
Jensen--Shannon divergence between each client's class prior and the
global class prior, computed per-seed (since Dirichlet partitions vary
per seed). The dose-response figure plots
$\Delta_{\mathrm{macro-F1}}$ against measured heterogeneity score,
with one point per (partition, seed) and the partition type as marker
shape.

If the drift-control mechanism is the operative cause of the FedProx
advantage, $\Delta$ should be monotonically non-decreasing in
measured heterogeneity, with the magnitude reflecting the
\emph{structure} of the heterogeneity (paired-co-training amplifies;
specialist single-owner does not). Departure from monotonicity, or
absence of an effect at high measured heterogeneity but presence at
the engineered partition, would be evidence that the mechanism is
specifically about paired co-training rather than generic label-skew
intensity. The four pre-registered outcomes of the specialist
sweep (\S\ref{sec:stat-het-specialist}) provide the inferential
framework for this distinction.

\subsection{Relationship to prior work}
\label{sec:stat-het-relwork}

The four-partition matrix extends prior FL evaluations in three ways:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{NIID-Bench} \citep{li2022niidbench} benchmarks seven
        algorithms across nine datasets and six partitioning
        protocols, but at three seeds and without a paired-seed
        inferential protocol; reported confidence intervals overlap
        on the closest comparable cell (DermaMNIST FedAvg vs.\
        FedProx). The present thesis adds the paired-seed protocol
        and statistical inference at $n=10$.
  \item The pre-registered specialist counterfactual is, to our
        knowledge, novel: prior work argues for or against bespoke
        partitions but does not run a structurally-matched alternative
        with quantity skew held constant.
\end{enumerate}

The results are reported in \S\ref{sec:results-statistical}. The
analytic pipeline (paired Wilcoxon, Hodges--Lehmann + exact
Walsh-average CI, sign test, per-class Holm correction, four-point
dose-response figure) is described in \S\ref{sec:metrics}.

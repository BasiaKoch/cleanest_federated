\section{System Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:results-system-het}

System heterogeneity refers to differences in the local computational
budget that participating clients contribute per communication round.
In production federated deployments this is the dominant operational
challenge: institutions run on heterogeneous hardware, on-call training
windows differ by site, and not all clients can complete the same
amount of local work within a round's wall-clock deadline. The proximal
term in FedProx was originally motivated for this regime by
\citet{li2020fedprox}: clients may submit $\gamma$-inexact updates ---
partial work that does not fully minimise the local objective --- while
the proximal anchor $(\mu/2)\lVert w - w^t \rVert^2$ provably preserves
convergence by preventing inexact updates from drifting too far from
the broadcast iterate $w^t$ (Theorem~4).

This section reports the FedProx-vs-FedAvg comparison under three
straggler conditions on the engineered \texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients}
partition (the same partition used as the primary in
Section~\ref{sec:results-statistical-het}) and additionally on the IID
partition (\texttt{iid\_7\_clients}) as a mechanism control. We follow
the same descriptive reporting convention as the
statistical-heterogeneity section: mean $\pm$ SD across paired seeds,
visual curve comparisons, bolded best results.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
We use ``system heterogeneity'' as shorthand for
\emph{computational-work heterogeneity}: variability in the number of
local SGD steps each client performs per round. The manipulation acts
only on the algorithm via heterogeneous local-epoch budgets $\tau_i$;
we do not simulate hardware speed, network latency, or device
availability.

\subsection{Experimental design and condition motivation}
\label{sec:system-het-conditions}

Three system-heterogeneity conditions are evaluated. Each schedule is
deterministic given the seed (see \texttt{fl/system\_het.py}), so
paired FedAvg/FedProx runs share an identical per-(round, client)
local-epoch assignment and the within-pair $\Delta$ reflects only the
algorithmic difference. The schedules under seed~42 are visualised in
Figure~\ref{fig:system-het-schedules}.

\paragraph{C0 (no system heterogeneity, baseline).}
Every client performs $E = 20$ local epochs every round. The design
is identical to the headline statistical-heterogeneity setup, but
re-executed in the Flower runtime so that all three system-het
conditions share a single runtime. C0 provides the within-runtime
baseline against which the system-het amplification can be assessed.

\paragraph{C1 (fixed stragglers).}
Two designated clients (C5 and C6) always perform
$E_{\text{straggler}} = 5$ local epochs; the remaining five clients
perform the full $E = 20$. Under the engineered partition, C5 holds
the melanoma/vascular specialty pair and C6 is the nevi-only
generalist, so the C1 design is structurally coupled with the
clinical-role assignment under that partition. Under the IID partition
the choice of (C5, C6) is arbitrary (clients are exchangeable), so the
IID C1 isolates the pure compute-heterogeneity effect more cleanly.

\paragraph{C2 (random stragglers).}
Each communication round, $\lfloor 0.5 \times 7 \rfloor + 1 = 4$
clients are randomly designated stragglers; each straggler performs
$E_i \sim \mathrm{Uniform}\{1, \dots, 19\}$ local epochs and the
remaining three perform $E = 20$. Aggregated across the ten seeds,
each client serves as a straggler in approximately $57\%$ of rounds.
This follows the protocol of \citet{li2020fedprox} §5.2 up to two
implementation details: stragglers always perform at least one
optimiser step (the Li et al.\ protocol allows $E_i = 0$), and both
algorithms include partial updates in aggregation (the Li et al.\
canonical FedAvg drops them; we recover the asymmetric protocol
separately in Section~\ref{sec:asymmetric}).

\subsection{Results on the engineered partition}
\label{sec:system-het-engineered}

Table~\ref{tab:system-het-headline} reports test macro-F1 under the
three conditions on the engineered partition. The mean within-pair
$\Delta$ rises monotonically from $+0.007$ at the no-system-het
baseline (C0) to $+0.013$ under fixed stragglers (C1) to $+0.017$
under random stragglers (C2). FedProx wins 7--8 of 10 paired seeds
in each condition. The pattern is consistent with the canonical
motivation of \citet{li2020fedprox}: as the amount of inexact local
work increases, the proximal regulariser's drift-control mechanism
has more drift to constrain and the within-pair advantage of FedProx
descriptively grows.

\input{T06_system_het_headline}

\paragraph{Per-class breakdown under C2.}
Table~\ref{tab:system-het-per-class} decomposes the C2 result by
class. The pattern matches that observed in the statistical-
heterogeneity section: the FedProx advantage is concentrated on the
melanoma class ($\Delta = +0.085$ macro-F1, FedProx wins 10/10
paired seeds), while the majority class melanocytic nevi shows
essentially zero difference. The proximal-anchor mechanism is
expected to be active only where inter-client drift exists, and the
melanoma class --- held by two specialist clients under the engineered
partition --- is exactly where drift is structurally produced.

\paragraph{Straggler-tolerance ratios.}
Table~\ref{tab:system-het-tolerance} reports the ratio
$\rho_a^c = M_a^c / M_a^{C0}$ of each algorithm's mean macro-F1 under

\subsection{Cross-partition control: IID partition}
\label{sec:system-het-iid}

To verify that the system-heterogeneity amplification observed on the
engineered partition reflects the proximal-anchor mechanism specifically
--- rather than a generic regularisation effect --- we ran the same
three conditions on the IID partition, in which every client receives
a uniform random sample of the global distribution and no inter-client
class skew exists. Under the proximal-anchor mechanism, the algorithm
has no drift to constrain on the IID partition and FedProx is
predicted to provide no benefit, irrespective of straggler condition.

Table~\ref{tab:system-het-iid} reports the IID-partition results. The
within-pair $\Delta$ values are small in absolute magnitude under all
three conditions ($-0.006$ at C0, $+0.010$ at C1, $-0.013$ at C2) and
not consistently signed across the three. The two algorithms' macro-F1
distributions overlap substantially on every condition. Whichever
straggler condition is applied, FedProx and FedAvg perform
indistinguishably on the IID partition --- consistent with the
proximal-anchor mechanism's null prediction.

\paragraph{Cross-partition comparison.}
Table~\ref{tab:system-het-cross-partition} contrasts the
within-pair $\Delta$ values between the two partition designs.
On the engineered partition, $\Delta$ is positive in all three
conditions and grows with straggler severity (mechanism active,
amplification visible). On the IID partition, $\Delta$ is small and
not consistently positive (mechanism inert). The pattern is exactly

Figure~\ref{fig:system-het-convergence} shows the trajectories
side-by-side: the top row (engineered) consistently displays a
visible FedProx-FedAvg gap, while the bottom row (IID) shows the

contrast between the two rows is the most direct illustration of
the mechanism's data-dependence: FedProx helps where there is class
skew, and is inert where there is not.

\subsection{Partial client participation}
\label{sec:partial-participation}

The conditions reported above vary the second canonical FL parameter
of system heterogeneity --- the local-epoch budget $E$ --- while
holding the per-round participation fraction fixed at $C = 1.0$ (every
client participates every round). The third canonical FL parameter,
$C$, is itself an axis of system heterogeneity widely tested in the
literature (\citealt{mcmahan2017fedavg} §3.1 varies
$C \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0\}$; \citealt{li2020fedprox} §5.3 tests
FedProx under partial participation on Synthetic and FEMNIST;
\citealt{kairouz2021advances} lists $C$ as a defining FL setup
parameter). In real federated deployments, not all clients respond
every round, so the full-participation setting reported above is the
cleanest baseline but the least operationally realistic case. We
additionally report a partial-participation condition at $C = 0.5$
(four of the seven clients sampled uniformly at random per round,
computed as $\max(1, \lfloor 0.5 \times 7 \rfloor) = 4$), holding all
other hyperparameters identical to the engineered C0 baseline.

\subsection{Replication of the canonical Li et al.\ §5.2 protocol}
\label{sec:asymmetric}

The H1/H2 tests above use a symmetric inclusion policy in which both
FedAvg and FedProx aggregate over all clients including stragglers.
The canonical asymmetric protocol of \citet{li2020fedprox} §5.2, in
contrast, has FedAvg drop straggler updates entirely while FedProx
includes them as $\gamma$-inexact partial work. The original paper
reports a substantial FedProx advantage under this asymmetric setup.

To verify that this canonical setup reproduces qualitatively on
DermaMNIST, we additionally ran the FedAvg-drop arm on the engineered
partition with random stragglers (the C2 schedule). Combined with the
existing FedProx-include arm from the symmetric C2 data on the same
ten seeds, this gives the within-pair comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:asymmetric-replication}.

\input{T09_asymmetric_decomposition}

The FedProx-include configuration outperforms FedAvg-drop by a mean
within-pair difference of $+0.068$ macro-F1, with FedProx winning
all 10 paired seeds. This reproduces the qualitative finding of
\citet{li2020fedprox} §5.2 on the DermaMNIST task: under the
canonical asymmetric straggler protocol, the FedProx configuration
produces a substantially higher macro-F1 than the FedAvg
configuration.

\paragraph{Scope and limitations.}
The within-pair $\Delta$ magnitudes on the engineered partition
($+0.007$--$+0.017$ macro-F1) are smaller than the effect sizes
originally reported in \citet{li2020fedprox} on Synthetic, FEMNIST,
Shakespeare, and Sentiment140 benchmarks. This is consistent with
the small absolute magnitude observed in the statistical-heterogeneity
arm under the same partition and runtime, and is plausibly attributable
to the smaller dataset, more challenging architecture, and
medical-imaging difficulty discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:headline-summary}. The C1 fixed-straggler condition
on the engineered partition is structurally coupled with the clinical
role assignment (C5 holds the melanoma/vascular pair); its descriptive
result should be read with that confound in mind. The asymmetric
protocol of Section~\ref{sec:asymmetric} provides a single point of
comparison against the canonical Li et al.\ setup but is not a primary
analysis; it is included to demonstrate qualitative reproducibility of
their §5.2 result on DermaMNIST. The straggler schedules are
deterministic functions of the seed and have been verified by direct
reconstruction from the JSON provenance --- the empirical per-client
straggler frequency, $E_i$ range, and round counts match the schedule
specifications exactly.

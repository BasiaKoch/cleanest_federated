\section{System Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:results-system-het}

System heterogeneity refers to differences in the local computational
budget that participating clients contribute per communication round.
In production federated deployments this is the dominant operational
challenge: hospitals run on heterogeneous hardware, on-call training
windows differ by site, and not all clients can complete the same
amount of local work within a round's wall-clock deadline. The
proximal term in FedProx was originally motivated for precisely this
regime (Li et al.\ 2020, Theorem~4): clients may submit
$\gamma$-inexact updates -- partial work that does not fully minimise
the local objective -- while the proximal anchor
$(\mu/2)\lVert w - w^t \rVert^2$ provably preserves convergence by
preventing inexact updates from drifting too far from the broadcast
iterate $w^t$. FedAvg has no such guarantee and treats partial-work
and full-work updates symmetrically under size-weighted aggregation.

This section tests whether the FedProx-vs-FedAvg advantage established
under statistical heterogeneity in Section~\ref{sec:results-statistical-het}
persists, attenuates, or amplifies when clients have heterogeneous
local-epoch budgets. We use the same engineered
\texttt{balanced\_paired\_7\_clients} partition, the same ten paired
seeds, and the same Flower simulation runtime throughout; the only
variable manipulated is the per-(round, client) local-epoch allocation.
A second strand of this section uses the canonical asymmetric
straggler protocol of Li et al.\ (2020) §5.2 to decompose the total
straggler-handling effect into a partial-work-inclusion component and
a residual proximal component, and includes an exploratory comparison
to FedNova, the specialised competitor algorithm for heterogeneous
local-step regimes.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
We use ``system heterogeneity'' as shorthand for what is strictly
\emph{computational-work heterogeneity}: variability in the number of
local SGD steps each client performs per round. We do not simulate
hardware speed, network latency, or device availability. The
manipulation acts only on the algorithm via heterogeneous local-epoch
budgets $\tau_i$. Following Li et al.~(2020)'s terminology is convenient
but should not be read as a hardware claim.

\subsection{Experimental design and condition motivation}
\label{sec:system-het-conditions}

Three system-heterogeneity conditions are evaluated. The schedules are
deterministic functions of the seed (see \texttt{fl/system\_het.py}),
so paired FedAvg/FedProx runs share an identical per-(round, client)
local-epoch assignment and the within-pair $\Delta$ reflects only the
algorithmic difference. The schedules under seed~42 are visualised in
Figure~\ref{fig:system-het-schedules}.

\paragraph{C0 (no system heterogeneity; baseline) -- runtime-matched reference.}
Every client performs $E = 20$ local epochs every round. The
\emph{design} is identical to the headline statistical-heterogeneity
sweep, but C0 is re-executed in the Flower runtime so that all three
conditions in this section share a single runtime. Mixing the
pure-PyTorch headline with Flower stragglers would have confounded the
system-heterogeneity manipulation with cross-runtime variance of
order~$\sim 0.02$ macro-F1 (cf.\ Section~\ref{sec:cross-runtime}).
Running C0 on Flower removes that confound and produces a
within-runtime baseline against which the H2 between-condition
contrast can be computed cleanly.

\paragraph{C1 (fixed stragglers) -- descriptive operational realism.}
Two designated clients (C5 and C6 in the partition) always perform
$E_{\text{straggler}} = 5$ local epochs; the remaining five clients
perform the full $E = 20$. This models a deployment in which certain
institutions consistently have weaker compute infrastructure. Under
this schedule the two designated clients are stragglers in every one
of the 150 rounds (the heatmap in
Figure~\ref{fig:system-het-schedules}b is a solid bar of $E = 5$ on
columns C5 and C6 throughout). C1 is retained for operational realism
but has a structural caveat: in our engineered partition, client~5 is
half of the melanoma/vascular specialty pair and client~6 is the
nevi-only generalist. C1 therefore conflates ``system heterogeneity''
with ``a particular clinical role configuration.'' We report C1
descriptively in the headline table; the clean isolation of the
system-heterogeneity effect rests on C2.

\paragraph{C2 (random stragglers) -- primary condition for inference.}
Each communication round, $\lfloor 0.5 \times 7 \rfloor + 1 = 4$
clients are randomly designated stragglers (sampled without
replacement). Each straggler performs $E_i \sim \mathrm{Uniform}\{1,
2, \dots, 19\}$ local epochs; the remaining three clients perform the
full $E = 20$. Aggregated across the ten paired seeds, every client
serves as a straggler in $\sim 57\%$ of rounds (per-client
range~$0.55$--$0.59$, verified by reconstructing the schedule from JSON
provenance), and the mean $E_i$ on straggler cells is $10.0$. This
follows the protocol of Li et al.\ (2020) §5.2 up to two implementation
deviations: (i)~we forbid $E_i = 0$ (a straggler always performs at
least one optimiser step), and (ii)~both algorithms include partial
updates in aggregation -- the canonical Li et al.\ FedAvg drops them.
The symmetric inclusion choice isolates the proximal-term effect from
the partial-work-inclusion effect; we then add the asymmetric protocol
in Section~\ref{sec:asymmetric} to recover the canonical Li et al.\
contrast and decompose the two effects per-seed.

\subsection{Pre-registered hypotheses}
\label{sec:system-het-hypotheses}

\paragraph{H1 (within-condition).}
For each condition $c \in \{\text{C1}, \text{C2}\}$, FedProx outperforms
FedAvg on test macro-F1 under paired Wilcoxon ($\alpha = 0.05$,
two-sided, $n = 10$). C0 is reported alongside for descriptive
reference; the within-runtime $\Delta$ at C0 is expected to be
non-negative but small (the engineered partition is fixed across
conditions, and the within-partition $\Delta$ on Flower is
known from Section~\ref{sec:cross-runtime} to be only $\sim 0.007$).

\paragraph{H2 (between-condition).}
Within-pair $\Delta$ is larger under system heterogeneity than at
baseline: $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_s^{c}] > \mathbb{E}[\Delta_s^{C0}]$ for
$c \in \{\text{C1}, \text{C2}\}$. Tested by paired Wilcoxon on the
per-seed difference $\Delta_s^{c} - \Delta_s^{C0}$, Bonferroni-corrected
across the two non-baseline conditions ($\alpha_{\text{family}} = 0.05$,
per-test threshold $0.025$). A positive significant result would
support the canonical FedProx claim that the proximal regulariser is
most beneficial under inexact local updates.

\subsection{Primary analysis: headline paired comparisons}
\label{sec:system-het-headline}

Table~\ref{tab:system-het-headline} reports the within-condition (H1)
and between-condition (H2) tests across the three conditions.

\input{tables/T06_system_het_headline}

The mean within-pair difference rises monotonically across conditions:
$\Delta_{C0} = +0.007$ at baseline, $\Delta_{C1} = +0.013$ under fixed
stragglers, $\Delta_{C2} = +0.017$ under random stragglers. The
direction is consistent with the pre-registered prediction (FedProx
benefits more under system heterogeneity), but none of the three H1
within-condition contrasts reaches $\alpha = 0.05$ at $n = 10$ paired
seeds (smallest $p_{H1} = 0.16$ at C1). The two H2 between-condition
contrasts are even further from significance (both Bonferroni-corrected
$p > 0.99$): although the mean $\Delta$ grows from C0 to C2, the
per-seed $\Delta$-of-$\Delta$ values vary in sign across the ten
paired seeds, so the test against zero is not informative at this
sample size.

The per-seed standard deviation of the $\Delta$ within each condition
is $\sim 0.03$ macro-F1; the smallest detectable mean-$\Delta$ at
$\alpha = 0.05$, $1-\beta = 0.8$, two-sided is approximately
$0.03 \cdot z_{0.8} / \sqrt{n} \cdot \sqrt{n}/n \approx 0.027$ for the
matched-pairs $t$-equivalent, so the observed C2 $\Delta = +0.017$ falls
into the underpowered region of the design. We therefore report the
H1 and H2 results as \emph{descriptively consistent with the
proximal-anchor mechanism but inferentially indeterminate at the
chosen sample size}. The H1 trajectories are shown for all three
conditions in
Figure~\ref{fig:system-het-convergence}; the visible separation
between the FedProx and FedAvg curves under both C1 and C2 -- modest
but stable from round~$\sim 30$ onwards -- is consistent with the
descriptive ordering of the $\Delta$ values.

\subsubsection{Per-class breakdown under C2}
\label{sec:system-het-per-class}

The per-class decomposition under C2
(Table~\ref{tab:system-het-per-class}, paired Wilcoxon with
Holm--Bonferroni correction over the family of seven classes) localises
the C2 advantage. Although the overall macro-F1 H1 contrast is not
significant, the per-class breakdown reveals that the melanoma class
\emph{is} Holm-corrected significant: $\Delta_{\text{melanoma}} = +0.085$,
$p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.014$, FedProx wins 10 of 10 paired seeds, raw
$p = 0.002$. This mirrors the per-class melanoma result from the
statistical-heterogeneity headline ($\Delta = +0.114$,
$p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.041$, Section~\ref{sec:per-class}) -- in both
heterogeneity regimes, the clinically critical melanoma class is the
single per-class anchor of the FedProx advantage. The other six
classes either show small positive non-significant differences
($+0.001$ to $+0.034$) or, in the vascular-lesions case, a small
non-significant negative difference ($-0.033$, raw $p = 0.13$).
Aggregating these heterogeneous per-class effects into a single
macro-F1 metric dilutes the melanoma signal below the H1 detection
threshold at $n = 10$, but the per-class evidence localises a
clinically meaningful FedProx benefit specifically on the
hardest minority class.

\subsection{Asymmetric straggler protocol and effect decomposition}
\label{sec:asymmetric}

The H1/H2 tests above use a symmetric inclusion policy (both FedAvg
and FedProx aggregate over all clients including stragglers), which is
the cleanest design for isolating the proximal-term effect from a
differential-participation confound. The canonical asymmetric protocol
of Li et al.\ (2020) §5.2, in contrast, has FedAvg \emph{drop}
straggler updates while FedProx \emph{includes} them, and reports a
large advantage to the latter. To recover this canonical contrast and
to decompose its total effect into a partial-work-inclusion component
and a residual proximal component, we run the additional asymmetric
arm in which FedAvg drops below-$E_{\max}$ updates. Combined with the
C2 symmetric data of the previous subsection, this provides three
algorithm-policy arms on the same ten seeds:
$(\text{FedAvg}, \text{drop})$, $(\text{FedAvg}, \text{include})$, and
$(\text{FedProx}, \text{include})$.

The three arms support the exact per-seed identity
$\Delta_{\text{total}} = \Delta_{\text{include}} + \Delta_{\text{proximal}}$
where
$\Delta_{\text{total}} = M_{\text{FedProx, include}} - M_{\text{FedAvg, drop}}$,
$\Delta_{\text{include}} = M_{\text{FedAvg, include}} - M_{\text{FedAvg, drop}}$,
and
$\Delta_{\text{proximal}} = M_{\text{FedProx, include}} - M_{\text{FedAvg, include}}$.
Table~\ref{tab:asymmetric-decomposition} reports the three components.

\input{tables/T09_asymmetric_decomposition}

The total effect $\Delta_{\text{total}} = +0.068$ (paired Wilcoxon
$p = 0.002$, 10 of 10 seeds positive) is large and statistically
robust; it recovers the qualitative form of the original Li et al.\
(2020) §5.2 result on DermaMNIST. The decomposition is interpretable:
$74.3\%$ of the total effect arises from \emph{including}
partial-work updates that the canonical FedAvg discards
($\Delta_{\text{include}} = +0.051$, $p = 0.006$), while $25.7\%$ is
the residual proximal-anchor effect on top of the inclusion choice
($\Delta_{\text{proximal}} = +0.017$, $p = 0.23$). The
per-seed identity error is bounded by floating-point precision (max
$< 10^{-10}$), so the share split is an exact algebraic
decomposition rather than an approximation.

The methodological lesson of this decomposition is that the canonical
Li et al.\ (2020) §5.2 ``FedProx beats FedAvg under stragglers''
result is, in the medical-imaging setting we evaluate, primarily a
result about \emph{aggregation policy} rather than a result about
\emph{the proximal term itself}. A practitioner reproducing the
canonical asymmetric setup would observe a $\sim 0.07$ macro-F1
advantage for FedProx and might conclude that the proximal anchor is
the operative mechanism, whereas in fact three-quarters of that
advantage would be recovered by simply changing FedAvg's aggregation
policy to include partial-work updates -- a software change that
requires no algorithmic change at all. The remaining quarter is
attributable to the proximal anchor but is not statistically
significant at $n = 10$; the decomposition therefore both confirms a
proximal effect of the expected sign and right-sizes the magnitude of
the contribution.

\subsection{Exploratory FedNova comparison}
\label{sec:fednova}

FedNova (Wang et al.\ 2020) is the specialised competitor to FedProx
for the heterogeneous local-step regime: its normalised-averaging
rule explicitly corrects for the objective inconsistency that arises
when clients perform different numbers of local SGD steps per round.
As an exploratory check we run FedNova under both C0 and C2 on the
same ten paired seeds.
Table~\ref{tab:fednova-comparison} reports the within-condition
contrasts; Figure~\ref{fig:system-het-convergence}d shows the
convergence trajectories.

\input{tables/T10_fednova_comparison}

Under C0 (no system heterogeneity), FedNova performs comparably to
FedAvg ($\Delta = -0.011$, $p = 0.16$) and is slightly outperformed by
FedProx ($\Delta = -0.018$, $p = 0.010$, descriptive). This is the
expected regime: with all clients performing equal local work, the
normalisation correction is a near-identity transformation and FedNova
collapses to a slightly noisier variant of FedAvg.

Under C2 (random stragglers), FedNova fails catastrophically: mean
macro-F1 drops to $0.24$ (against $0.49$ for FedAvg and $0.51$ for
FedProx under the same schedule), within-pair differences against
FedAvg and FedProx are both $\approx -0.26$ at $p = 0.002$ (10/10
losses), and the convergence trajectory in
Figure~\ref{fig:system-het-convergence}d plateaus near the
all-nevi-baseline level (the dataset is $67\%$ nevi, so a constant
prediction of nevi yields macro-F1~$\sim 0.13$ but accuracy~$\sim 0.67$;
the FedNova C2 trajectory sits modestly above this floor).

The plausible mechanism is interaction between FedNova's
normalisation rule and the highly heterogeneous local-epoch budgets
under C2. FedNova rescales each client update by the ratio of its
local steps to the population-average steps, then aggregates the
rescaled updates. When some clients have $\tau_i = 1$ while others
have $\tau_i = 20$, this rescaling amplifies the noisy small-step
updates and attenuates the high-quality large-step updates, dragging
the aggregated update toward the noisy direction. The schedule
sanity-check in Section~\ref{sec:system-het-sanity} confirms that
FedNova-C2 update norms are $\sim 25\%$ smaller on straggler cells
than non-straggler cells, consistent with this hypothesis. We report
this as a substantive negative finding for FedNova in the
heterogeneous medical-imaging regime: the algorithm specifically
designed for objective-inconsistency under heterogeneous local
steps performs markedly worse than both FedAvg and FedProx in
the only condition where its corrective machinery is supposed to
apply.

\subsection{Straggler-tolerance ratios}
\label{sec:tolerance}

To quantify each algorithm's robustness to system heterogeneity
relative to its no-system-het baseline,
Table~\ref{tab:system-het-tolerance} reports the ratio
$\rho_a^c = M_a^c / M_a^{C0}$ for each algorithm $a$ and condition $c$.
Both FedAvg and FedProx maintain $\rho \approx 1$ under C1 and C2:
FedAvg's $\rho$ ranges from $0.993$ (C2) to $1.003$ (C1) and FedProx's
ranges from $1.014$ (C2) to $1.016$ (C1). The $\rho > 1$ values are
implausible as substantive claims (it is not realistic that adding
stragglers improves performance) and are best read as evidence that
the baseline-vs-condition differences are within the within-runtime
seed-noise floor of $\sim 0.005$ macro-F1 at $n = 10$.

\input{tables/T08_straggler_tolerance}

The substantively useful comparison in this table is between FedAvg
and FedProx within each row: FedProx's $\rho$ is descriptively higher

\subsection{Schedule sanity verification}
\label{sec:system-het-sanity}

Before interpreting any of the system-heterogeneity results we
verified that the executed schedules actually correspond to the
designed conditions. The schedules are deterministic functions of the
seed, so we reconstruct each seed's per-(round, client) schedule from
JSON provenance and verify the aggregate statistics.

Under C1, only clients C5 and C6 ever serve as stragglers; each is a
straggler in $1500 / 1500 = 100\%$ of (seed, round) aggregated cells;
all straggler cells have $E_i = 5$ exactly. The update-norm ratio
$\lVert \Delta w_{\text{straggler}} \rVert / \lVert \Delta w_{\text{non-straggler}} \rVert = 0.673$
is consistent with stragglers performing $5/20 = 25\%$ of the work
producing smaller (but not proportionally smaller, since the optimiser
state accumulates) parameter updates.

Under C2, all seven clients serve as stragglers; the per-client
straggler frequency lies in $[0.552, 0.592]$ (target $4/7 = 0.571$),
$E_i$ on straggler cells has range $[1, 19]$ with mean $10.0$ (target
$10.0$ for $U\{1, \dots, 19\}$), and the update-norm ratio is
$1.009$ -- stragglers and non-stragglers produce updates of
essentially equal magnitude on average, because the random schedule
mixes long and short epoch counts and the within-cell variance
dominates the cross-cell mean.

Under FedNova-C2 the update-norm ratio is $0.754$, reflecting the
normalised averaging rule: small-step updates are rescaled and emerge
smaller in normalised aggregation. As noted in
Section~\ref{sec:fednova}, this rescaling appears to be the operative
mechanism in FedNova's catastrophic C2 collapse.

\subsection{Summary and limitations of the system-heterogeneity arm}
\label{sec:system-het-summary}

\paragraph{Summary.}
Under symmetric inclusion (both algorithms include stragglers in
aggregation), the within-condition FedProx-vs-FedAvg $\Delta$ rises
monotonically from $+0.007$ at C0 to $+0.013$ at C1 to $+0.017$ at C2,
but none of the three within-condition contrasts reach $\alpha = 0.05$
at $n = 10$ paired seeds. The per-class breakdown under the primary
C2 condition recovers the headline statistical-heterogeneity per-class
finding: melanoma is the single Holm-corrected significant class
($\Delta = +0.085$, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.014$, 10/10 wins), with the
remaining classes showing small non-significant differences.

Under the canonical asymmetric protocol of Li et al.\ (2020) §5.2,
the total FedProx-vs-FedAvg-drop effect of $+0.068$ macro-F1 ($p = 0.002$,
10/10 wins) decomposes per-seed into a $74.3\%$ partial-work-inclusion
component and a $25.7\%$ residual proximal-anchor component, with the
identity satisfied to floating-point precision. This decomposition
right-sizes the canonical ``FedProx wins under stragglers'' result:
three-quarters of it is attributable to aggregation-policy choice
rather than to the proximal regulariser, and the remaining
proximal-anchor effect is positive but not significant at the chosen
sample size.

The exploratory FedNova comparison reveals a substantive negative
finding for the specialised competitor algorithm: FedNova performs
comparably to FedAvg under no-system-het but collapses catastrophically
under random stragglers (macro-F1~$0.24$ vs $0.49$/$0.51$ for
FedAvg/FedProx, both within-condition $\Delta$ values $\approx -0.26$
at $p = 0.002$). FedProx is therefore the more robust of the two
straggler-aware algorithms in the medical-imaging regime we evaluate.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The H1/H2 sample-size analysis (Section~\ref{sec:system-het-headline})
indicates that the design is underpowered for the magnitudes of
$\Delta$ that the C2 condition actually produces; on the basis of the
observed within-seed standard deviation, formal H1 significance at
$\alpha = 0.05$ would require approximately $n \gtrsim 25$ paired
seeds for the C2 contrast. The asymmetric decomposition's residual
proximal effect ($\Delta_{\text{proximal}} = +0.017$) falls in
the same underpowered region; only the partial-work-inclusion
component is statistically significant.

The C1 fixed-straggler condition is structurally confounded with the
engineered-partition's clinical-role assignment (Section~\ref{sec:system-het-conditions});
its result should be read as ``the particular configuration in which
the melanoma/vascular specialist and the nevi generalist are slow,''
not as a clean isolation of the system-heterogeneity effect. C2 is
the cleanly-isolating condition.

The asymmetric-protocol arm uses the same ten paired seeds as the
symmetric C2 arm but introduces an additional FedAvg-drop arm; the
per-seed identity $\Delta_{\text{total}} = \Delta_{\text{include}} +
\Delta_{\text{proximal}}$ is algebraic and is verified exactly, so the
decomposition is not an interpretive choice but a property of the
data.

The FedNova C2 result depends on the specific definition of
normalised aggregation in Wang et al.\ (2020); we use the formulation
implemented in our Flower-runtime client, which matches the canonical
formula. Variants of FedNova that handle partial work via different
normalisation schemes (e.g.\ FedNova-M with momentum correction) are
not evaluated and may behave differently.
